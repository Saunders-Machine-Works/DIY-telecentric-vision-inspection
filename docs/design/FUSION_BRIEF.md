## 1. What we are building

A single, rigid, 3D-printed body (printed in a few sections that bolt and dowel together into one
rigid assembly) that holds a backlit telecentric vision rig **in line**:

```
backlight -> tunnel -> [part station, focal plane] -> focal distance (air) -> lens -> camera
```

The rig measures turned parts (pins, threaded shafts) from their silhouette. Today it is about
700 mm (27 in) long and is several loose printed parts. John wants it consolidated into one
design. **It stays in line. No mirrors, no folded paths.** (A folded layout was studied and
rejected: the lens side is a rigid 463 mm and mirrors add risk to a measurement that is good to
about 1 micrometre. Do not propose mirrors.)

The honest expectation: consolidating will not make it much shorter. The optics fix about 700 mm.
The gains are rigidity, alignment that stays put, one thing to move, and built-in features.

### Decisions already made by John
- In line, no mirrors.
- Use the file that is open in Fusion. **Edit it by adding NEW components. When you are done
  using an old component, turn its visibility off (do not delete it).** Tell John which old
  components you turned off.
- Most existing parts (lens, camera, backlight, reticle, old bases, etc.) are already modeled in
  that file and are accurate enough to design around. Work out which is which from the file.
- The tunnel is NOT in the file yet. Import it (Step 2). It was generated by a script and its
  STEP files are on this machine (see section 5).
- Vertical part orientation is the primary mode. A person must be able to swap the rig to
  horizontal parts (section 4, feature 9).
- Part size to hold and measure: **up to 0.6 in (15.24 mm) wide and 1.5 in (38.1 mm) tall.**
- Bench mounting was left to us to decide (section 4, feature 8).

## 2. Ground rules (how to work in this Fusion file)

- Treat the document as a messy real one. Read it before touching it: units, component tree,
  timeline length, existing parameters, joints.
- Use the Fusion CAD skill if you have it. If not, follow the same discipline: parametric, named,
  verified (the points below).
- Work in **new components**. Name them clearly. Put the new design under one top-level new
  component, for example `Rig_v2`, with sub-components per printed section.
- Drive the design from **named user parameters** (section 6). Never hard-code a number that is
  in that list.
- Internal API units are centimetres and radians. Use string values with units
  (`"157.8 mm"`), not bare numbers.
- Never open a modal dialog. Never swallow an exception. Verify after every feature
  (`healthState`, body counts, bounding boxes) and look at a screenshot.
- Do not switch to direct modeling.
- **Gate on structure once** (Step 1): component tree, parameters, feature order. Wait for John's
  go-ahead, then build without asking again. If a structural assumption turns out wrong, stop and
  say so.
- Flag real manufacturing problems (print problems) in the same breath as the work, not in an
  end-of-job summary. Real means it will fail or cause trouble on the printer. Stay quiet about
  merely suboptimal things.
- Build in increments: one feature group per script. If a script fails halfway, repair or undo to
  a clean state. Never stack a second attempt on a partial first one.

## 3. The optics and what must be true

All numbers in mm unless stated. Optical axis is a straight line through everything.

| Item | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lens | Opto Engineering TC12056 bi-telecentric, 0.114x | Body 205 mm front to C-mount flange. Take the true outline and front diameter from the model in the file. |
| Focal distance | **157.8** from the lens FRONT face to the part axis | Depth of field is only 31.9 mm, so the part must sit within about +-10 mm of this. |
| Field of view | about 70 mm circle; sensor gives about 74 x 62 mm | Parts are small; the point is the position, not the size. |
| Camera | Basler acA2440-20gm, GigE, C-mount | Take outline and connector positions from the model. Allow cable bend room. |
| Backlight | Advanced Illumination CX0404-455C1 (4 in x 4 in), blue | Take thickness and outline from the model. Controller: SignaTech DCS-100E (external). |
| Tunnel | 200 mm long, bore 30 x 80, slot 24 x 70 | Imported in Step 2. A collimator: it makes the light a narrow cone (about +-3.4 deg across the part). The 200 mm length IS the collimation. Do not shorten it. |
| Reticle | PT-series chrome-on-glass chessboard, 0.6 mm squares | Take size from the model. It is swapped in and out at the part position for calibration. |

### Alignment needs (these are why a single rigid body is worth it)
- The part axis, the slot centre, the lens axis and the camera sensor centre must all lie on the
  optical axis, with the **part centred in the slot**. An off-centre part makes the two background
  margins unequal and the measurement drifts (a 5 percent brightness ramp moved a 0.5 in pin
  reading by 6 micrometres). So: provide **centring adjustment** (feature 2).
- The part's focal plane must be adjustable to hit 157.8 +- 10 mm from the lens front (feature 3).
- Light must reach the lens only through the part station. Everything between the tunnel exit and
  the lens front is a light-controlled space: dark, black, flock-ready (feature 6).
- The tunnel exit to part gap was 5 mm on the old rig. Keep it small (5 to 10 mm).

## 4. Features the design must have

1. **Reticle gate.** A slit in the focal plane, through the part's axis, so the chessboard reticle
   drops in and out without disturbing anything. It must seat the glass plate square to the
   optical axis, repeatably (a ledge plus a spring clip or two screws). Size it from the reticle
   model.
2. **Centring adjustment.** Fine-pitch screws (M4 or M5) move the **part holder** and the **slot
   plate / tunnel** sideways about +-3 mm, then lock. Adjustment is across the optical axis,
   horizontal. The goal is equal bright margins on both sides of the part.
3. **Focus adjustment.** The lens/camera carriage OR the part station slides along the axis about
   +-10 mm on a printed dovetail or rail and locks with a screw. Choose whichever is stiffer; say
   which and why at the gate.
4. **Lens saddles.** Two printed cradles with straps, so the lens carries its own weight and the
   camera is not hanging off the C-mount. Check the lens outline from the model; allow 0.3 mm
   clearance on the bore.
5. **Camera cradle with cable strain relief.** GigE and I/O cable exits at the rear; keep cables
   from pulling on the camera.
6. **Light-tight shroud.** Black PETG; interior surfaces of the tunnel and the space between part
   station and lens front must be accessible for flocking. A lid over the part station. **The top
   must stay open enough for a part to stand up to 1.5 in (38.1 mm) above the holder.**
7. **Electronics bay.** Mounts for the backlight controller (DCS-100E) and a power supply, with
   cable channels in the base. Ask John for the controller and supply sizes if they are not in
   the file.
8. **Bench mounting (our decision, John said TBD).** Proposal, adjust if the geometry argues
   otherwise: a recessed pocket for a 16 mm diameter, 3 mm thick self-adhesive rubber foot at each
   corner of each printed section (so the rig sits on rubber, not on rough print surface), plus
   two 1/4-20 heat-set inserts on the underside centreline of the lens section (for a tripod head,
   optical post or clamp), 50 mm apart. Keep the underside flat otherwise.
9. **Vertical / horizontal swap.**
   - Vertical mode (primary): the part stands on a holder (feature 10). The slot is 24 wide
     (across the part) by 70 tall (along it).
   - Horizontal mode: the slot must be turned 90 degrees so its narrow 24 mm side lies across the
     part's diameter. If the slot is wrong for the part orientation, the light cone along the
     measured width is about +-9 degrees and the diameter reads about 0.0007 in small. This is not
     optional for horizontal parts.
   - The tunnel bore is 30 x 80, so a slot plate alone cannot rotate. The simplest honest
     solution is to let the **whole light head (backlight + tunnel) rotate 90 degrees** about the
     optical axis in two round support collars, locked by screws, with the holder plug swapped for
     a V-block pair. Propose this or something simpler at the gate. If it complicates the base
     badly, propose building vertical-only in this pass and leaving the collars in place so
     horizontal can be added without redesign.
10. **Part holder.** Holds a part up to 15.24 mm wide and 38.1 mm tall, vertical, standing on its
    end. The held foot must stay BELOW the lit field so the measured length is bare (the scan
    window is roughly the middle of the field). A flat seat with a V-notch facing the lens and a
    light spring clip is a good start. It plugs into a seat on the part station (dovetail or
    pins), with the centring adjustment from feature 2. Design the holder so it can be swapped
    for a V-block carrier.
11. **Sections.** Each printed section at most about **300 mm** long, wide and tall (the H2D bed is
    about 325 x 320 x 325). Join sections with dovetail or tongue-and-groove plus two 3 mm steel
    dowel pins at each joint and M4 screws into heat-set inserts. Put joints where no optical
    path crosses. Aim for three sections: (a) backlight + tunnel + part station, (b) focal-distance
    bridge + lens saddles, (c) camera cradle. Say at the gate if you see a better split.
12. **Stiffness.** The assembly must stay aligned. Use ribbed or box-section construction with
    walls of at least 2.4 mm (6 perimeters at 0.4 mm), and 3 to 4 mm around inserts and screw
    bosses.

## 8. Why these numbers (so you can judge edge cases)

- The telecentric lens has no perspective error but a shallow depth of field (31.9 mm). Focus
  position matters; the part must sit near the focal plane.
- The tunnel collimates the light. A wider cone makes a polished part read small (a specular
  rim); that is why the tunnel is 200 mm long for a 24 mm slot. A shorter tunnel needs a narrower
  slot, which cannot light a 15 mm part's edges.
- A horizontal part needs the narrow slot side across its diameter, which is why the light head
  must turn.
- The measurement software warns when the background brightness beside the part differs by more
  than 3 percent. That is the practical alignment test, so make centring easy.
- Accuracy is the first priority (+-0.0005 in target; about 0.00005 in on certified pins today).
  Stability of alignment is worth more than any cosmetic or weight saving.
